# Deep learning, optimal transport and foundation models for program2cell: research plan (2026-11-16)

*Status 2026-11-16: plan only, nothing run. Not part of Paper 1. Freeze v2 stays the Paper 1 method
(`method_spec.md`). This plan refines Paper 2 (`research_plan.md` §4) and sits next to PALM-uni
(`palm_uni_plan.md`), sharing its simulator but not its held-out block.*

## 1. Question and short answer

Should program2cell use deep learning (DL), optimal transport (OT), transformers or single-cell / bulk foundation
models (FMs)?

| Family | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Amortized simulation-based inference** (neural posterior estimation with a set transformer over samples) | **Yes, main line of Paper 2** | Targets our actual weak points: calibration under misspecification (W1, W2, W10), speed at atlas scale, and a richer model than a tractable likelihood allows (the PALM-uni R2 terms) |
| **BulkFormer encoder** (bulk FM, already in Paper 2) | **Yes, as an ablation-gated component** | Only kept if it beats the same head without the encoder on real bulk; it addresses W6 (platform) at best |
| **DL deconvolution** (Scaden, TAPE, Kassandra-type) as the fraction module | **Cheap test, may help W6** | PALM already takes any method's fractions (`fraction_draws`, `research_plan.md` §3.8); one afternoon to plug in |
| **Single-cell FMs** (scGPT, Geneformer, scFoundation, UCE, State) as priors for type-specific program footprints | **Low prior odds, one fast kill-test** | Independent benchmarks find zero-shot FM embeddings and perturbation predictions do not beat simple linear baselines; W7 needs footprints that the data can check |
| **Optimal transport** (CellOT, CINEMA-OT, CellFlow) | **Narrow uses only** | Bulk data are means; OT's strength is distributions of single cells. Two uses survive: reference-to-bulk domain alignment (W6) and generating heterogeneous within-type responses for the training simulator (W2) |

What none of these can do: recover what bulk does not identify. Near-identical types (W4) and causal attribution
between cell types stay unidentifiable; a network trained on a prior will return that prior there. Every learned
estimator below is therefore scored on the same safety rules as freeze v2 (null calls, coverage), not on accuracy
alone.

## 2. Why amortized inference fits this problem and FMs mostly do not

Our estimand is the change β in program activity per cell type, estimated *from the cohort's own bulk data*. We
do not need to predict responses to unmeasured perturbations, which is what most FM and OT perturbation work does.
The weak points of freeze v2 (`weak_points_plan_2026-11-12.md` §2) are about inference:

| Weak point | What fails | What a learned estimator can change |
|---|---|---|
| W1 intervals too wide on the calibrated route | width from the simulated-error quantile | posterior learned directly on data like ours; calibrated by simulation-based calibration (SBC) |
| W2 leakage residue on non-responders | model-to-data gap; every widening costs power | put responder/non-responder mixtures and reference donor variance into the *training simulator*; no tractable likelihood needed |
| W4 unstable unmixing | identifiability | nothing; the posterior should stay wide there (a check, not a fix) |
| W6 platform bias in fractions | reference ≠ bulk | train on real bulk too (BulkFormer, Teacher 2) or align with OT |
| W10 router selects on the data it reports | two estimators plus a guard | one estimator, no route choice |
| Speed (OneK1K fits ~280 s; atlas scale impossible) | NUTS / two-stage per cohort | one forward pass per cohort |

The October joint model failed through misspecification (biased β, intervals too narrow; `palm_uni_plan.md`).
Neural posterior estimation (NPE) does not remove misspecification: it moves the problem into the simulator.
The gain is that the simulator can include terms that have no closed-form likelihood, and that calibration can be
checked at scale before any real data are touched. Robust-NPE methods exist for the residual gap (§6).

## 3. Design of the main estimator (PALM-amortized)

**Input: the existing stage-1 summaries, not raw genes.** For each sample i the two-stage model already computes
Gaussian summaries (x̂ᵢ, Λᵢ) of fractions and program shifts (`bayes/twostage.py`, `sample_summaries`). Using
them as tokens:
- keeps every quantity defined by the shared core equations (the two-paper rule: Paper 2 must not redefine
  quantities);
- cuts the dimension from ~2,000 genes to C + C·P numbers plus a precision;
- transports across gene panels and program sets better than raw counts.
A raw-gene variant (with the BulkFormer encoder) is an ablation, not the default.

**Network.** A set transformer over samples (samples are exchangeable given the design; cohorts vary from 6 to 300
samples): token = (x̂ᵢ, Cholesky of Λᵢ, covariate zᵢ, batch), pooled by attention, then a conditional normalizing
flow over β (C × P). Output: posterior draws of β; calls and intervals as in freeze v2. Stack: JAX (equinox +
flowjax, or BayesFlow with the JAX backend) to stay on the current stack; PyTorch only to load BulkFormer weights.

**Training data, three teachers** (the first two extend `research_plan.md` §4.3):

| Teacher | Source | Truth | Teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 simulator | `sim.simulate_causal` + PALM-uni terms: reference donor variance, off-program residual, responder fraction per type, RNA content, platform offset | exact β | calibration; misspecification we can name |
| T1 atlas pseudobulk | Tahoe-100M, Parse 10M PBMC cytokines, CELLxGENE Census (incl. OneK1K-type cohorts) | β measured on the pooled cells, same definition as the benchmarks | the real model-to-data gap |
| T2 real bulk | ARCHS4 / GTEx blood, reconstruction through the core equations | none (self-supervised) | what real bulk looks like (W6) |

**Prior.** An amortized posterior is the posterior under the training distribution of β. That distribution must
be a spike-and-slab with most pairs null (as in the benchmarks) or the network will call nulls. The β prior is
fixed before training and reported; a sensitivity run with a half-as-sparse prior is part of every gate.

**Leakage rules.** Our dev benchmarks use Tahoe, Parse, Kang and OneK1K. T1 training excludes: the benchmark drugs
(encorafenib, gemcitabine, RMC-6236, trametinib, idasanutlin) and their target-class neighbours; the 5 benchmark
cytokines and the whole type-I/II IFN family (Kang IFN-β is the same biology as Parse IFN-β); the benchmark cell
lines; OneK1K donors. COMBAT, GSE60424 and the in-house paired cohort are never used in training.

## 4. Experiments (development only; each a go/no-go)

Scored with `causal_loop.py` on the 13 development datasets, same metrics and rules as the PALM-uni U-series: S1
coverage ≥ 0.90 per dataset; null calls ≤ freeze v2 + 0.02; power compared with v2 and v5; no step kept for a gain
inside split noise (0.03). Development splits 0–2 and 20–29 only.

| Step | What | Data | Go if | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D0** | DL deconvolution as the fraction module: Scaden and TAPE (deep ensemble of 5 → fraction draws) into `program_deconvolution(fraction_draws=…)`, vs InstaPrism | 13 dev datasets, splits 0–2; COMBAT (CyTOF fractions) | fraction RMSE better than InstaPrism on ≥ 2/3 of datasets **and** S1/S2 hold | ~2 days, CPU |
| **D1** | PALM-amortized on T0 only, *current* model as simulator (no new terms): checks the machinery | sim, then Kang, McFarland, Zhang splits 0–2 | SBC ranks uniform (χ² p > 0.01 per parameter class); real-data rules as U0; matches the two-stage posterior on the same simulator (β mean |Δ| < 0.05) | ~1 week code, 1 GPU-day |
| **D2** | T0 with misspecification terms (donor variance, off-program residual, responder mixtures, platform) | 13 dev datasets, splits 20–29 | McFarland null calls < v2's 0.05 / 0.08 (W2) with power ≥ v2; S1 on every dataset | ~1 week, 2–3 GPU-days |
| **D3** | add T1 atlas pseudobulk; leave-perturbation-out split | as D2 + transport set: 9 never-run perturbations (v4 lane e) | null calls on unseen perturbations ≤ v2 + 0.02; coverage ≥ 0.90 on unseen perturbations | ~2 weeks, data pipeline is the bulk of it |
| **D4** | BulkFormer encoder + T2, vs D3 without encoder | COMBAT, GSE60424 (paired sorted cells), in-house paired cohort | beats D3 on real bulk (fractions vs CyTOF; β vs sorted-cell truth) by > split noise; else drop the encoder | ~3 weeks, 1 GPU |
| **D5** | FM footprints: type-specific program gene weights from scGPT / Geneformer / State in-silico perturbation, vs (a) the shared footprint now used and (b) footprints estimated by pseudobulk regression in the reference | Kang, Parse, PTB programs (W7) | beats **(b)** on null calls and power; otherwise kill (expected) | ~1 week |
| **D6** | OT, two narrow uses: (a) entropic OT alignment of reference profiles to bulk space vs current platform offsets; (b) CellFlow/CellOT counterfactual cells to augment T1, only if D3 is data-limited (learning curve still rising at full T1) | (a) COMBAT, GSE60424; (b) as D3 | (a) fraction error below offsets by > noise; (b) transport gain > 0.03 | (a) ~3 days, (b) ~2 weeks |

**Order.** D0 and D1 first, in parallel (independent, cheap, D1 is the foundation of everything else). D2 waits
for the PALM-uni U0 result (the SMI/tempering run now on development splits): if tempering alone fixes
calibration, D2's main argument becomes speed and the W2 terms, and its simulator terms are taken from U1–U2
instead of being designed twice. D5 and D6 run only if a person-week is free; neither blocks Paper 2.

**Stop rules.** Stop the amortized line if D1 cannot reach SBC uniformity on its own simulator (a machinery
problem we should not carry into real data), or if D2 fails S1 on any dataset after two simulator revisions. Then
Paper 2 falls back to the fast two-stage model on GPU, which already has a fixed definition and needs no training.

## 5. Held-out confirmation

Nothing above touches held-out splits. Before any held-out run, a protocol in the style of `method_spec.md` is
written and frozen: the estimator, the training data manifest (with the leakage exclusions of §3 as a checked
list), the β prior, and the decision rule. Proposed block: **splits 70–79**, not yet confirmed unused; 30–39 stay
reserved for v5, 50–59 for the association section, 60–69 proposed for PALM-uni. Real-bulk confirmation on
COMBAT and GSE60424, which no step trains on.

## 6. Risks

| Risk | Sign | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Network returns the training prior where bulk is uninformative (W4) | narrow intervals on near-identical types | SBC per parameter class; report posterior-to-prior width ratio; never claim attribution there |
| Simulator gap on real data | coverage fine on T0, poor on T1 / real | robust NPE: learned misspecification-robust summaries (Huang et al. 2023), a misspecification detector in summary space (Schmitt et al. 2023), conservative ensembles (Hermans et al. 2022) |
| Leakage from atlas training | unusually good scores on IFN or MEK/KRAS benchmarks | exclusion list checked in code; leave-family-out run |
| Overconfidence after training | coverage < 0.90 on unseen perturbations | conformal recalibration of interval width on a held-out T1 calibration set |
| FM priors look good on in-distribution programs only | D5 gains vanish on PTB programs | treat as killed; FMs are not on the critical path |
| Compute | T1 pseudobulk from 100M cells | pseudobulk once to disk (per perturbation × line × replicate); training from summaries is small |

## 7. Paper framing

- **Paper 1** is unchanged: the calibrated Bayesian method (freeze v2), written as a cut model
  (`methods_cut_model.md`).
- **Paper 2** becomes "amortized, calibrated cell-type program inference for any bulk cohort": Paper 1's model and
  definitions, a set-transformer posterior trained on simulator + atlas pseudobulk, checked by SBC and on real bulk
  with truth, run on GTEx / TCGA / ARCHS4 for the atlas (`research_plan.md` §4.5). The BulkFormer encoder and FM
  priors appear only if D4 / D5 pass, otherwise as negative results in the supplement, which is itself useful
  given the field's FM benchmarks.
